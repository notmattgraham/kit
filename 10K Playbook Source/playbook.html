The math, worked out

If 5 out of every 1,000 people who watch your videos follow you, 10,000 followers takes about 2,000,000 total views. Across 100 videos that's an average of 20,000 views per video.

That sounds like a lot until you see how it really goes. Most of your videos will do a few hundred or a few thousand views. Two or three will be outliers and do the heavy lifting. One 500,000-view video is a quarter of the whole job.

The whole game
You don't control which video breaks out. You control how many swings you take, how much better each swing gets, and whether people have a reason to follow when one finally connects.

The power of volume

If you take one thing from this entire playbook, take this page. Volume beats luck. One video can get unlucky. A hundred videos can't. Every time you post, you get another swing, and organic content gives you unlimited swings for free.

Two ways onto someone's feed

Paid

You pay with money.

Run ads and you buy your placement on people's feeds. Stop paying and you disappear.

Organic

You pay with skill.

There's no way to buy organic reach. You earn your spot by getting good. And skill is the one thing nobody can take from you.

How you build skill
Skill = Volume × Improvement

Times, not plus. If either one is zero, so is your skill.

Skill and hit rate

Your hit rate is the percentage of your videos that become outliers. Skill and hit rate are directly correlated. The higher your skill, the fewer videos you have to post to get an outlier. The lower your skill, the more videos it takes.

Volume × improvement→More skill→Higher hit rate→Followers come faster

As your skill goes up, it gets easier to go viral, and your followers grow faster. Early on, you're paying for skill with volume. Every video you post with a clear intention to improve makes the next outlier cheaper.

10,000 is the hard part

The five rules

Everything in this playbook sits on top of these. Break one and the rest stops working.

1

One platform

Pick TikTok, Instagram Reels, or YouTube Shorts and build there. Pick the one you already scroll the most, because you already know what works on it. You can repost to the others later. You build on one.

If you want my pick: Instagram. It's the easiest of the three to make money on. Brand deals, DMs that turn into clients, and selling your own offers all happen there more naturally than anywhere else.

2

At least one video a day

One a day is the floor, not the ceiling. If you can do two to five, do two to five.

3

New videos only

No reposting the same video to fish for views. Every day is a new idea, a new hook, a new attempt.

It costs you more than one video. Adam Mosseri, the head of Instagram, has said Instagram ranks original content above reposts, and accounts that keep reposting are less likely to get recommended at all. One lazy repost can drag down the reach of everything you post after it.

4

Get better every video

Everyone says consistency is the key. Iteration is the key. Posting the same bad video 100 times gets you nowhere. Every video, pick at least one thing to do better than the last one. The people who win pick more than one.

5

No verdicts before video 100

Your first 30 videos are tuition. You don't get to decide "this isn't working" at video 12. You get to decide at video 100, with real data in front of you.

Proof it works
Matt grew from 300 to over 250,000 followers in three months. Joe went from 0 to 11,000 followers in 30 days. Same rules: one platform, a new video every day, better every time.

Width and depth

Building an audience takes two different jobs. Width brings new people into your orbit. Depth turns those people into a real relationship. You'll need both eventually. To get to 10,000 followers, width comes first.

Width

Cast a wide net.

Get in front of people who have never heard of you. This is what grows your follower count.

Best format: short-form video.

Depth

Deepen the relationship.

Help the people already in your orbit get to know who you are, so they trust you.

Best formats: photos, carousels, stories, lives, long-form, replies and DMs.

Where each format fits
FormatMain jobWhy
Short-form videoWidthIt's what the apps push hardest to people who don't follow you yet.
Photos and carouselsDepthYour followers get more of you: your thinking, your life, your story.
Stories and livesDepthMostly seen by people who already follow you. That's where trust gets built.
Replies and DMsDepthOne-on-one conversations turn followers into fans.
Why depth matters later

Depth is what gets people to take action. Width gets them in the door. Depth gets them to join your email list, buy your product, or show up to your event. Without it, you have an audience that watches but never does anything.

For your first 10,000
Prioritize width. Your daily video is the non-negotiable. Photos, carousels, and stories are a bonus on top of it, never a replacement for it.

Kill the excuses first

You don't need to feel ready. You need to stop letting these six thoughts decide for you.

ExcusePeople I know will judge me
Some will. Look at who's laughing. It's never the people with lives you want. A millionaire doesn't laugh at someone starting their first business. Post it anyway. Their reaction isn't a requirement.
ExcuseI'm not interesting
Your life feels boring to you because you live it every day. You don't get to decide what a stranger finds interesting. The video does. Make it and let them decide.
ExcuseNobody will watch
Good. A small audience is a free lab. You get to experiment while nobody's paying attention and you have no brand to protect. Treat every video as a test, not a verdict.
ExcuseI need better gear
The phone in your pocket shoots in 4K. Window light, clear audio, a clear point. That's the whole kit. Use what you have.
ExcuseWho am I to talk about this?
A blue belt can teach a white belt. Someone is stuck on a problem you've already solved. If you haven't solved it yet, document the process. Teach what you've done, or show what you're doing.
ExcuseIt's too late. It's too saturated.
Saturated with mediocre, maybe. A crowded lane means the demand is proven. Nobody can out-compete you at getting better faster. Make a video, then make the next one better.

Step 06 / Views

Win the first three seconds

If they swipe in the first three seconds, nothing else you did matters. Every hook has three layers. Use all three in every video.

What they see

Movement or something unexpected in the very first frame. Start mid-action. No walking into frame, no "hey guys."

What they hear

A first sentence that raises a question they need answered. Specific beats clever.

What they read

Text on screen in frame one that says what the video is. A lot of people scroll on mute.

Eight hooks you can fill in tonight
1"Stop [common habit] if you want [result]."5"Here's how to [result] without [thing they hate]."
2"I [did surprising thing] for [time]. Here's what happened."6"I was [bad situation] until I figured out [this]."
3"The reason you [problem] isn't [what they think]."7"You're [doing common thing] wrong."
4"[Number] things I wish I knew before [milestone]."8"This is what [result] actually looks like."
Hook rules
  • Be specific. Numbers, names, money, days. "I lost 22 pounds in 90 days" beats "my weight loss journey."
  • Make a claim, don't announce one. Cut "what I'm about to tell you will change your life." Just say the thing.
  • Attack a belief. Telling people something new is fine. Telling them what they believe is wrong is better.
  • The first frame is the thumbnail. If the first frame is boring, the video is boring.
Every video
Write three hooks. Read them out loud. Post the one you'd stop scrolling for.

Step 07 / Views

Hook, retain, reward

The hook raises a question. The middle builds toward the answer. The ending pays it off. People who watch to the end are the reason the app shows your video to more people.

Hook / raise a specific question

The first three seconds make them need the answer. How? What happened? What does the other side look like?

Example: "Here's how I lost 22 pounds without a gym." The question: how?

Retain / build toward the answer

Context, a story, or an example that keeps moving them toward the payoff. Where you started, what you tried, what didn't work.

Example: "I know this because here's where I was in March."

Reward / deliver the answer

The lesson, the reveal, the one change that made the difference. Answer exactly what your hook asked.

Example: The one change that caused the 22 pounds.
Five retention rules
  • No intro. Your first sentence is the hook. Your second sentence is already the content.
  • Hold the answer. The moment they get what they came for, they leave. Give it at the end.
  • Change something every few seconds. A cut, a zoom, a new line of text, a new point.
  • Keep it short. Start between 20 and 45 seconds. Earn the right to go longer.
  • End on the punch. No "so yeah, that's it." Last line lands, video ends.
A full example (about 30 seconds)
Hook"I lost 22 pounds this year and I never once set foot in a gym." On screen: 22 LBS. ZERO GYM.
Retain"In January I was 214 pounds, eating drive-thru twice a day. I tried running. Quit. Tried a meal plan. Quit. Everything I tried needed me to become a different person overnight."
Reward"What worked was one rule: I don't eat anything I didn't make. Cooking takes long enough that the snacking stopped on its own. 22 pounds."
Follow"If that helped, follow. I post one of these every day."

Step 08 / Production

The 60-minute daily workflow

You're doing this 100 times. If one video takes four hours, you'll quit by video 15. Here's how to make one in an hour.

10MIN

Plan

Pull an idea from your bank. Write three hooks and pick one. Write the 3 to 5 points you'll hit, or the full script. Never do your thinking on camera.

15MIN

Film

Face a window so the light is in front of you. Phone at eye level, lens wiped, quiet room. Look at the lens, not yourself. Record one line at a time if you need to.

25MIN

Edit

Cut every pause, breath, and "um." Captions on every word, spelled right. Zoom in on your key lines. Make sure the first frame is the most interesting frame.

5MIN

Watch it once as a stranger

Watch it like it just showed up in your feed. The second you get bored or want to swipe, cut that part.

5MIN

Post

Write a caption that adds context and uses the words someone would search for. Post. Then stay for the first hour (Step 09).

Talking to a camera
  • It'll feel awkward. You'll think you sound stupid. Everyone does at first. Post it anyway. It stops feeling weird around video 20.
  • Talk like you're on FaceTime with one friend. Use the words you'd actually say. Skip the presenter voice.
Buy yourself time
Film three videos on Sunday and you've already covered Monday and Tuesday. Batching is how people with jobs post every day.

Step 09 / Follow rate

Give them a reason to follow

A view is a stranger. A follower is someone who wants the next one. Most creators get views and wonder why nobody follows. It's because they never gave anyone a reason.

Four follow triggers

Pin your intro post

When someone taps your profile, the first thing they see should tell them who you are and why to stick around.

Make every video worth it

If this video gave them real value, they assume the next one will too. One weak video makes them doubt the follow.

Stay in their world

Adjacent topics are great. Random ones aren't. A video your person wouldn't care about tells them the next one might not be for them either.

Show your personality

People follow people. The tips get the view. You get the follow.

Ask the right way

"Follow for more" is wallpaper. Nobody hears it anymore. Give a specific reason: "I post one of these every single day." "Follow if you're trying to [result] this year."

The first hour after you post
  • Reply to every comment. Conversations bring people back to the video and make commenters feel seen.
  • Turn good comments into videos. Reply to a comment with a video. It's a free idea, and the commenter will probably share it.
  • Spend 15 minutes commenting in your lane. Leave real comments on other creators' videos, not "great video." People click on good commenters.
Once you're past 1,000

Find 5 creators your size in your lane and make something together: a collab post, a duet, a stitch, a back and forth. Their audience already likes your topic. It's the cheapest growth there is.

Step 11 / Iteration

Double down on what works

Most creators get an outlier, celebrate, and go right back to posting whatever. That's the most expensive mistake in this whole playbook. A winner is the audience telling you exactly what to make next.

The outlier rule

Every outlier earns three follow-ups within seven days:

1

Follow-up

Make a video that answers the most common question in the comments.

2

Same format, new topic

Keep the structure, the hook style, and the length. Swap the subject.

3

Same topic, new hook

Say the same thing from a different angle. Winners can win more than once.

The five-strike rule

If a pillar or format flops five times in a row, even after you fixed the hook, retire it and replace it with something new.

The plan

Zero to 10,000 in three phases

Each phase has one job and one checkpoint. If you hit the checkpoint, move on. If you don't, the fix is written right there.

Phase 1 / 0 to 1,000
Videos 1 to 30

The job: reps. Get comfortable on camera, lock in who you're talking to, and post every single day. Don't stare at your follower count. Ask one question: is this video better than the last one?

Checkpoint at video 30: one to three videos clearly beat the rest. If not: your hooks are the problem. Redo Step 06 and pick topics more people care about.

Phase 2 / 1,000 to 5,000
Videos 31 to 70

The job: find your format. Apply the outlier rule every time. Retire what's not working. Start commenting and collabing with creators your size.

Checkpoint at video 70: you know which pillar and which format gets you the most follows, and you're making more of it. If views are fine but follows are stuck: Step 01, Step 02, Step 09.

Phase 3 / 5,000 to 10,000
Videos 71 to 100+

The job: scale what works. 80% of your videos are formats you've already proven. 20% are experiments. Pin your best three. Collab every week.

Checkpoint at video 100: 10,000 followers, or a clear, written diagnosis of exactly which number is holding you back and what you're doing about it.

The honest part
Some people hit 10,000 at video 20. Some hit it at video 150. The phases are a guide, not a deadline. Follow the rules, fix what the numbers tell you to fix, and the only way to lose is to stop.
